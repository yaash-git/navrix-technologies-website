Pricing approach

Clarity on the work
before the quote.

No price list or fixed packages are published here. A responsible proposal depends on understanding the outcomes, scope, constraints, and responsibilities involved.

An enquiry-led conversation

Start with the shape of the problem.

Project cost can change significantly with the product surface, technical context, content needs, integrations, review process, and ongoing support. We don’t invent a rate or a range to make that uncertainty look precise.

When inquiry delivery is configured, the first step can help establish what you need, what is already in place, and what still needs to be learned. If the work is a fit, a written proposal can describe the scope and assumptions for discussion.

There are no published prices, package rates, or delivery promises on this site. Any future quote should be confirmed directly by Navrix Technologies.

How scope gets clearer

Three steps toward a grounded estimate.

The process makes room to discover what is not yet known before the work is priced.

Understand the need

Clarify who the product serves, what it should help them do, and what constraints shape the work.

Define the work

Agree the deliverables, responsibilities, integrations, review points, and decisions that belong in scope.

Review the proposal

Discuss assumptions, exclusions, schedule dependencies, and possible support so expectations are explicit.

What influences scope

Useful detail beats a misleading package.

These are common considerations, not a checklist every project must satisfy. The relevant factors depend on the problem and your team’s environment.

  • Number and complexity of user journeys
  • Existing services, APIs, or legacy systems
  • Content creation, migration, or review needs
  • Accessibility, security, and quality requirements
  • Who will make decisions and provide feedback
  • Expected ownership and post-launch support
