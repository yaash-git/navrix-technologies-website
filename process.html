From first question to ongoing care

Seven connected parts of the journey.

The sequence can flex to fit the scope. Each step has a purpose, an output to discuss, and a decision about what should happen next.

01 / DISCOVERY

Listen & learn

Understand users, stakeholders, current workflows, and the conditions around the challenge.

People · context · constraints
02 / PLANNING

Set direction

Agree on goals, priorities, scope, responsibilities, risks, and how progress will be reviewed.

Goals · scope · plan
03 / DESIGN

Shape the experience

Map information and journeys, explore interaction patterns, and prototype important decisions.

Structure · flows · prototypes
04 / DEVELOPMENT

Build in increments

Implement the agreed direction in reviewable parts, keeping code quality and ownership in view.

Implementation · integration
05 / TESTING

Check the details

Review expected behavior, accessibility basics, responsive layouts, content, and edge cases.

Quality · usability · readiness
06 / LAUNCH

Prepare & release

Confirm the release checklist, ownership, handover, and the steps needed to make the product available.

Release · handover · documentation
07 / SUPPORT

Learn & improve

Plan for maintenance and decide how future changes should be evaluated and prioritized.

Support · feedback · iteration
How collaboration stays clear

Good process is useful in the day-to-day.

Visible work

Keep current questions, decisions, and in-progress work accessible to the people responsible for them.

Small review points

Review direction at useful intervals so feedback can shape the next increment instead of arriving at the end.

Clear assumptions

Record what is known, what remains uncertain, and which decisions depend on new information.

Ownership in view

Consider who will operate, maintain, and evolve the solution as part of planning and handover.

Questions about the process

Start at the stage that fits.

Can a project start with discovery only?

Yes. A focused discovery can clarify the opportunity, relevant constraints, and possible next steps before a larger commitment is made.

Does every engagement include all seven stages?

No. The stages describe a complete journey. A scoped design review or planning engagement may focus on only part of it.

How are testing and launch handled?

Testing and release responsibilities should be agreed during planning. The exact checks depend on the product, integrations, and the teams involved.

What happens after launch?

Maintenance and improvement needs can be scoped separately. Support arrangements are discussed before they are represented as part of a proposal.
